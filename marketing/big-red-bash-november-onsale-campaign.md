**Three readings, one of them counter-intuitive:**

1. **The event mechanic beat everything else we ran.** 2.74× blended against 0.79% on retargeting and 1.20× on the Club launch. Event CTRs of 5.25–8.32% sit well clear of the 4% kill line in the creative deck.
2. **The furthest-away event sold best.** Tablelands was the #1 product by revenue in the whole store for the period and effectively sold out. Lakeside — closest to the depot, best delivery position, overnight express — sold worst and still holds 27 units. **Planning horizon drove sales, not delivery speed.** Every instinct said Wee Jasper was our strongest position. The data says otherwise, and I had that wrong.
3. **Scale was where the money was.** Tablelands LP took 50k impressions and 18 of the 39 purchases. The small-budget Core ad sets produced lovely CTR and zero sales.

**Why that matters here:** Birdsville is the most extreme version of the thing that worked. Longest planning horizon of any event we have touched, hardest to resupply, most deliberate packing decision anyone makes. Point 2 says that is exactly the condition these packs sell into.

## 2. The naming constraint — read this first

All three event packs have been **renamed to strip the festival brand names**, and the names are gone from the page copy too:

| Was | Now |
|---|---|
| The Deni Pack — … for the Ute Muster | The Muster Pack — … for the Long Weekend |
| The Savannah Pack — … for Mareeba | The Tablelands Pack — … for Far North Queensland |
| The Lakeside Pack — … for Dragon Dreaming | The Lakeside Pack — … for a Weekend by the Lake |

Checked against the live pages: "Savannah in the Round", "Deni Ute Muster", "Dragon Dreaming", "Kerribee" and "Deniliquin" return **zero** hits across all three. "Wee Jasper" survives 24 times on the Lakeside page, and the non-affiliation fine print is still on all three.

**The pattern is unambiguous: event brand names are out, place names are in.** Wee Jasper is a locality; Dragon Dreaming is a trademark.

So this campaign runs on **place and fact, never the event brand**. That kills the name-dominant creative format in `event-ad-creative-deck.md`, where the festival name was deliberately the largest element in the frame. It cannot be used here.

**No real loss.** The place is just as unmistakable to someone holding a ticket, and the facts are better copy:

- ✅ Birdsville · the Simpson Desert · the dune · far south-west Queensland · July 2027
- ❌ Big Red Bash · "the final Bash" · Bashville · any organiser slogan

## 4. Timing — three beats, and the first one is the campaign

The instinct is to campaign in June 2027. That wastes nine months of runway. The high-intent moment is on-sale, when people are buying tickets, booking leave, costing fuel and thinking about what goes in the vehicle.

| Beat | When | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **1. On-sale** | **Mon 9 – Thu 26 Nov 2026** | The campaign. Be there while they plan the trip. |
| 2. Instalment | from Mon 1 Mar 2027 | They are paying for the trip again and re-engaged. Light touch. |
| 3. Pack the car | from Fri 4 Jun 2027 | Order-by messaging. The Canberra dispatch note belongs here, not in November. |

### Beat 1 in detail

| Date | |
|---|---|
| Mon 9 Nov | Product live, page live, Meta campaigns built but **paused** |
| Wed 11 Nov | Teaser email. No hard sell — "tickets tomorrow, here's the packing problem" |
| **Thu 12 Nov, 10am AEDT** | **Tickets on sale.** Enable Meta at 10am sharp. The highest-intent 48 hours of the whole nine months |

| Thu 19 Nov | Second email to non-openers, different angle |

Deposits mean nobody is paying the full ticket price on day one, so there is room for a $159 pack in the same week.

## 5. Creative

Format carries over from the October deck — **the event's own rule is the scroll-stopper** — minus the brand name. Place leads instead.

**Name block, fixed across variants:** `BIRDSVILLE` / `JULY 2027 · SIMPSON DESERT`

Same construction: enormous condensed white caps on a flat black band, small cyan date line beneath, hook on a band across the bottom. Big type on flat colour renders reliably; type over a busy photo is what produced the butchered labels first time round.

| | Hook line | Offer line |
|---|---|---|
| **A — lead** | THERE IS NO BAR. THERE IS NO SHOP. | 12 GLASS-FREE COCKTAILS · FREE SHIPPING |
| B | WHAT YOU PACK IN JULY IS WHAT YOU DRINK IN JULY | NOTHING SOLD ON SITE · FREE SHIPPING |
| C | THE NEAREST BOTTLE SHOP IS THE BIRDSVILLE PUB | PLAN ACCORDINGLY · FREE SHIPPING |
| D — on-sale week | YOU'VE GOT THE TICKET. NOW THE ESKY. | ORDER NOW, IT'S WAITING WHEN YOU PACK |

**Primary text (lead):**

> No alcohol is sold anywhere on site. The nearest bottle shop is the Birdsville Pub, and that is a long way back down the track.
>
> So whatever goes in the vehicle in your driveway is what you are drinking in the desert. Glass is banned outright — the only glass anyone wants out there is the condiment jars in the camp kitchen.
>
> Twelve bar-made cocktails in resealable pouches. Glass-free, crush-proof, one shelf of the esky, and they clip to a lanyard so you can carry one down to the dune with both hands free.
>
> Order now and it is sitting at home waiting when you load the car in winter.

**Headline:** There is no bar in the Simpson Desert
**Description:** Glass-free. Free shipping. $159.
**CTA:** Shop now

**The concert-area rule is the real differentiator** and no other event we have run has it: BYO is allowed **in the concert area**. At the Muster nothing goes into the Arena; at the Tablelands the precinct is bone dry. Here you carry your own drink to the stage — which makes the lanyard pitch relevant across the whole festival rather than just back at camp. Worth its own variant once the lead is proven.

### Image direction

Standing rules apply: one element, pouch large in frame, left hand with the spout at the lips, no `image_references` alongside an element, and look at the reference images before prompting.

Scene: red desert sand, low scrub, a 4WD or troop carrier with a roof rack, swags, an esky on the tailgate, long winter shadows. **Hats and jackets, not singlets** — July in the Simpson is cold overnight and mild by day. Getting that wrong will read as fake to anyone who has been. No campfire.

## 7. Meta structure

Mirror what worked. LP campaigns did the volume and the money; small-budget Core ad sets produced high CTR and no sales.

| Campaign | Budget | Targeting |
|---|---|---|
| `MXT_DesertPack_2027_Sales_LP` | $60/day | **National**, 30–65. Interests: outback travel, 4WD and caravanning, camping, Australian country and classic rock. This is the main one. |
| `MXT_DesertPack_2027_Sales_Core` | $20/day | Radius on the feeder towns and drive-in routes — Mount Isa, Longreach, Charleville, Broken Hill, Port Augusta |

Objective `OUTCOME_SALES`, `OFFSITE_CONVERSIONS` on `IMPRESSIONS`, `promoted_object {pixel_id: 723291006083300, custom_event_type: PURCHASE}`, page `137867822740681`. Set campaign `bid_strategy` to `LOWEST_COST_WITHOUT_CAP` **before** creating ad sets, or the ad set create fails asking for a bid amount.

**Targeting is national, not geographic.** Nobody gets delivery at Birdsville; they drive for days and the pack goes to a home address. That removes our transit constraint entirely — the inverse of the Tablelands campaign, where Far North Queensland transit was the binding constraint on everything.

## 8. Inventory and risk

**Do not over-commit.** The 2026 event was cancelled outright, the site had about six feet of floodwater on it, and the organisers expect it dry by late February 2027 on the Bureau's evaporation modelling. Ticketholders get a full refund if it cannot go ahead. **We would not.**

Two mitigations are already built in: the SKU name survives the event, and the twelve pouches are the same physical pool as every other pack — the three October packs were 60 units against one pool, badged three ways. So allocating units to the Desert Pack costs nothing real.

Suggested opening allocation: **20 units**, matching the October pattern, with Tablelands' sell-out as evidence that is about the right size for a single event push.

Separately: **41 units sit unsold in Lakeside (27) and Muster (13)** — more than this campaign is likely to move. The renames presumably exist to keep them selling. Worth its own conversation.
